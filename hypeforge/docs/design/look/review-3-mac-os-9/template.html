M-1 · The menu bar

These menus, in this order?

Emblem · App name · Workspaces · Favorites · Window · Special · Help … clock · Application menu. Mac OS 9 put each app's own File and Edit menus here; Sway can't, so our menus take their place.

my pick: as drawn

M-2 · Title bars

Real title bars on every window?

The Mac OS 9 look: a bold, centred title, the focused one lighter. Each window loses 20 px of height. Off means borders only, like today.

my pick: on

M-3 · The Control Strip

Status icons in the Control Strip?

The tray, clipboard, drives, Wi-Fi, sound, night light, screens and the bell move into the bottom-left strip, which folds away with its tab. The menu bar stays clean, the way Mac OS 9 kept it.

my pick: the Control Strip

M-4 · The font

Noto Sans bold, or a Charcoal look-alike?

Mac OS 9's Charcoal font is Apple's; nog finds no free look-alike packaged today. Bold Noto Sans comes close; a look-alike would mean packaging a free font ourselves.

my pick: Noto Sans bold

M-5 · The shadow

Mac OS 9's crisp shadow?

On the SwayFX login, windows and menus get the small hard shadow Mac OS 9 had, down and to the right. Plain Sway: menus keep theirs, windows sit flat. Flip "Look" to compare.

my pick: crisp shadow with SwayFX

Honest limits

What this Mac OS 9 won't have

Not on Sway

  • Each app's own File, Edit and View menus in the menu bar: apps keep their menus inside their windows.
  • The title bar's pinstripes and 3D edges, and its close, zoom and collapse boxes: Sway draws one flat colour and the title.
  • WindowShade (double-click a title to roll the window up).
  • Icons on the desktop (the hard disk, the Trash): Sway draws no desktop icons.

Different on purpose

  • Windows tile instead of overlapping; float one with Win + Shift + Space.
  • The emblem menu is the launcher: your groups as submenus, Favorites and recent apps on top.
  • The Control Strip holds the modern things Mac OS 9 never had (tray, clipboard, notifications).
