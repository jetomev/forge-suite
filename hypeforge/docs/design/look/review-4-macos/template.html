macOS, tiled: a frosted menu bar, the Dock, Spotlight and Control Center

Today's macOS look on our tiling desktop. The menu bar keeps what you approved for Mac OS 9 (the emblem menu, the bold app name, Workspaces, Favorites, Window), now frosted and soft. The Dock sits at the bottom with your favourites and what's running. Spotlight is today's launcher in a new coat, and Control Center, Launchpad and Notification Center open from the bar. Windows still tile above the Dock. Click the icons at the top right of the screen below.

What I need from you

Five calls, and anything that feels off

My pick is marked on each. Tell me in your own words what doesn't feel like macOS.

MA-1 · The Dock

Always there, or hidden until you reach for it?

Always there: a second Waybar, nothing to install, windows tile above it. Hidden until the mouse touches the bottom edge needs nwg-dock (5 MB, Arch's own repository), stopped and started by exact name.

my pick: always there

MA-2 · Finding apps

Spotlight and Launchpad, both?

Spotlight is today's launcher, restyled as a wide rounded bar in the middle (works today). Launchpad is a full-screen grid of every app with your groups as tabs, our panel (to build).

my pick: both

MA-3 · Control Center

These blocks?

Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and VPN in one block; Do Not Disturb; Night light (nightForge); Screens (displayForge); the sound slider; what's playing (Spotify and friends). The same tiles you approved for Windows 11, macOS-shaped.

my pick: these

MA-4 · The menu bar

Mac OS 9's menus, kept?

Emblem menu · bold app name · Workspaces · Favorites · Window · Help, so the two Mac styles feel like one family. Special moves into the emblem menu, the way modern macOS has it.

my pick: kept

MA-5 · Frosted glass

See-through bar, Dock and panels on SwayFX?

macOS's "vibrancy": the wallpaper shows softly through the menu bar, the Dock and every panel. Plain Sway: the same shapes, solid. Flip "Look" to compare.

my pick: frosted on SwayFX

Honest limits

What this macOS won't have

Not on Sway

  • Each app's own menus in the menu bar (same as Mac OS 9).
  • The Dock's magnifying wave when the mouse moves over it.
  • The red, yellow and green buttons on every window.
  • Mission Control's live picture of every window; the Workspaces panel shows which apps are where instead.
  • Minimize into the Dock (an app can be put away in the hidden drawer instead).

Different on purpose

  • Windows tile; the Dock never covers them.
  • Launchpad opens on your groups, and an app still opens in its own workspace.
  • No animations (SwayFX #565 and #569).
