workspaceForge, screen by screen

Your workspaces, in the terminal: their names and order, which apps open on each one, and which screens keep the same apps across workspaces. Every screen is drawn here before any code is written.

Today all of this lives in a settings file you'd have to edit by hand. workspaceForge is the friendly way in. It follows displayForge on purpose: the same frame, the same menu bar with underlined letters, a list on the left, the picked thing on the right, and a review before every save. Every drawing is exactly 100 columns wide. The drawings show your six workspaces, your three screens and the apps on this desktop today.

"approved!!!!" The third draft below is the design. The eleven questions go with the recommendations (D-5): as many workspaces as you like, at least one; one app, one workspace; Find and Category filters on the apps table; one shared group per screen; "Select All / Deselect All" everywhere; deleting moves windows, never closes them; the lists start from the launcher's groups; F10 saves after a review and applies at once; the Workspaces applet does the work; a Workspaces page in hypeForge Settings; 0.1.0 while it's built, 1.0.0 after your run.

Already decided · Javier, 9 October

  1. Workspaces only. "Notice I said only workspaces, windows is another Forge app. I like atomized solutions." Where windows go on a screen (the fill order) and which windows float become their own Forge app later.
  2. Apps open on their workspace however you start them (F-50, #55): typed in the launcher, Favorites, the bar, a terminal, or Steam starting a game.
  3. The screens go with the app when it opens on another workspace.
  4. Shared screens need no special rule: their windows already belong to every workspace in the group, and the fill order counts them.
  5. Workspaces are yours to shape (first draft review): "If the user just want to have one, they only have one." Create, name, edit and remove them as you please, from one up.
  6. Apps is two tables with arrows between them (first draft review): apps on no workspace on the left with [x] ticks; your workspaces on the right, one open at a time showing its apps; >> sends the ticked ones to the open workspace, << sends them back. Select All and Deselect All on top of each table, acting only on the rows showing.
  7. Workspaces, second review: New, Edit and Delete on top of the page, Move Up and Move Down on top of the list. New and Edit happen right on the page, no pop-up; Delete keeps its warning window. After a rename, a short yellow ⚠ note asks you to check the workspace's apps; it goes away on its own.
  8. Apps: "perfect."
  9. Sharing is a grid of switches: the same table, with an Own / Shared switch in every cell instead of text, and a Save button at the bottom.

Questions for you

Each one has my recommendation in bold. Say "go" and they're all answered that way; change any you like. The first draft's "up to nine" question is replaced by #1.

  1. More than nine? You can have as many as you like, but Win + 1 … 9 only reach the first nine; the rest are one click away in the bar's Workspaces list. And at least one always stays (the last one can be renamed, not deleted). Recommended: yes.
  2. One app, one workspace. Your two tables already work that way: an app sits either on the left (no workspace) or in one workspace. To move Discord from Daily to Gaming: open Daily, send it back with <<, open Gaming, send it with >>. Recommended: yes.
  3. Filters on the apps table (top right, as for every table): a Find box and a Category list (All, Games, Utilities…). Headings sort with a click, ▲ ▼ showing which way. Recommended: these two. Any other filter you'd want?
  4. Sharing, one group per screen: with a switch per cell, every cell switched to Shared in a column shares that screen together. So each screen has at most one shared group. Your applet could do more (two separate groups on one screen), but nobody needs that, and the switches stay simple. Recommended: one group per screen.
  5. The words: "Select All" and "Deselect All" are yours. nogForge says "Tick All" and "Untick All" for the same thing. Recommended: yours everywhere; nogForge follows in its next release.
  6. Deleting a workspace that has windows open: it asks where they go (Daily picked for you). Nothing is ever closed. Recommended: yes.
  7. The lists start from the launcher's groups, once: Internet on Daily (11 apps), Office and Development on Work (15), Multimedia on Entertainment (6), Games on Gaming (7), the monitors on Monitoring (5), Settings on Settings (14); the other 26 open where you are. After that the lists are the only place this is set, and the launcher follows them too. Recommended: yes. Heads-up: Discord, Dropbox, Insync and the VPN start by themselves at login. They'll go to Daily quietly, without moving your screens.
  8. Saving: F10 shows a review, then it applies at once, no logout. No countdown like displayForge's: nothing here can black out a screen. Recommended: yes.
  9. Who does the work: hypeForge's Workspaces applet, which already runs all the time, moves the windows. workspaceForge only edits its settings file and tells it to re-read. Your workspaces keep working with workspaceForge closed, or even uninstalled. Recommended: yes.
  10. Inside hypeForge Settings: a "Workspaces" page that opens workspaceForge, like Screens opens displayForge. Recommended: yes.
  11. Version: 0.1.0 while it's built; 1.0.0 when this whole design works and you've run it, like displayForge. Recommended: yes.

1 · Workspaces

Yours to shape: one workspace or a dozen, any names, any order. The buttons sit on top: Move Up and Move Down over the list (moving one changes its Win number), New, Edit and Delete over the page. Your workspaces down the left, with the Win key that reaches each; ● marks the one on screen now. On the right, the picked one: its name, how many apps open there, whether it shares a screen, and what's open on it right now. At the bottom, the main switch: workspaces that span every screen, or Sway's own way, where each screen switches by itself.

Editing a workspace

No pop-up either: Edit makes the Name field on the page editable, Enter keeps it, Esc puts it back. After a rename, the apps line turns yellow with a ⚠ for a moment: the apps that opened on "Entertainment" now open on "Media", and that's worth a look on the Apps page. The note isn't kept: it goes away when you pick another workspace or leave the page.

2 · Apps

Your layout. On the left, every app that's on no workspace yet (it opens wherever you are), in our table style: ticks, its category, every other row shaded, headings that sort, and the filters top right. On the right, your workspaces. Click one and it opens downward to show its apps; click another and the first one closes. Between them, >> sends the ticked apps from the left into the open workspace, and << sends the ticked ones from the open workspace back to the left. Select All and Deselect All tick only the rows you can see: the filtered list on the left, the open workspace on the right.

In the drawing, the left list is filtered to Utilities (16 of the 26 apps on no workspace), Winetricks is ticked, and Gaming is open. One press of >> and Winetricks opens on Gaming from then on. The counts are real: what this desktop's launcher would put on each workspace today.

3 · Sharing

Workspaces down, screens across, and a switch in every cell: Own or Shared. Flip the cells of a screen to Shared in the workspaces that should keep the same apps on it. In the drawing, the left screen is switched to Shared in Daily, Work and Entertainment: whatever you put on the left screen (your chat, your music) stays there in those three, while the main and right screens change as you switch. One cell alone shares nothing; it takes two or more in a column. Nothing changes until you press Save at the bottom. Today nothing is shared.

Saving

A review first: every change, before and after. A backup of the file is made, then it's written, and the Workspaces applet re-reads it right away. Open windows don't move; a renamed workspace keeps its windows.

Keys

The same rules as every Forge app: Ctrl + the underlined letter, or the number in menu order, Help included.

How it gets built

  1. Your answers to the questions above, and any change to the drawings.
  2. The engine first (F-50): the Workspaces applet learns the app lists and moves new windows to their workspace, screens following. It works with the settings file edited by hand, so you can live with it before the app exists.
  3. workspaceForge on forgekit, as a new section of the Forge Suite (workspaceforge/): Workspaces, Apps, Sharing, the review and save, then its page inside hypeForge Settings.
  4. Tests: one, two and three screens; 100 columns; a plain text console; a broken or missing settings file; each check proven failing first.
  5. Your run with a written test list, then a release on GitHub, and the AUR after your local test.

Until then your workspaces keep working exactly as they do now.
